# Campaign

Filled in at the close of session zero, so that no later session has to ask
again.

- **System:** Starfinder Second Edition (Player Core, GM Core, Tech Core, Alien Core).
- **Started from:** character creation (session zero, 2026-10-13). Level 1. Opens in Kita's apartment *before* any crew has found her.
- **Rules skill in use:** `starfinder-2e-tech-core` (its `search.py` covers all four books). Look rules up; don't trust memory.
- **House rules and table conventions:**
  - **Drone control range is ignored.** Kita controls Miku at any distance (RAW 150 ft). The friction comes from what Miku *can't* do — weak skills, no hands for some jobs, fragile — and from jobs that need Kita physically present. Make that friction real. Modify still needs Kita adjacent to Miku.
  - **Acting through Miku:** what Kita does *through* Miku (talking via speakers, perceiving via cameras, knowledge, judgement) uses Kita's modifiers. What Miku's *body* does (Stealth, Acrobatics, attacks, fine manipulation incl. Thievery) uses Miku's. Miku can't hack a terminal for her until the Network (tactical) customization.
  - **Standard robot customizations** (Speakers, Manual Dexterity, Skill Module…) can fill a drone's commercial customization slots.
  - **Anxiety has no mechanics.** The player roleplays it. The GM may apply an ad hoc circumstance penalty when her state would plausibly impair a check; the player explicitly OK'd that.
  - The player often steps out of character to explain what they're after — welcome it.

## Table commands

For the player. Written here so that nobody has to be told during a session.

- **"stop" or "skip that"** — said out of character at any point. Obeyed
  immediately, no questions asked. To undo something that already happened, use
  the client's own rollback; it's better at that than the GM is.
- **`log: <anything>`** — appended verbatim to `playtests/current.md` and play
  carries on. A note for whoever edits the GM's instructions later, not a
  conversation. Delete `playtests/` if this campaign isn't a test.

## The long shape

**What the player wants (their words, near-verbatim — check sessions against this):**

- **Doing:** hacking and infiltration jobs are the best fit; mercenary one-off jobs are fine. Kita "isn't a thief, but Starfinder is the kinda place where you can be a thief and a good person, because there are evil corporations and cults around" — evil in the literal sense ("we literally worship Satan, and want to send people to hell").
- **Relationships** (Nib, Odessa, Lolo, tessellate, her parents) "will be relevant to anything, so I don't think they should be the focus." Texture and pressure, not plot.
- **Tone:** lightness and pulp welcome — "the world doesn't need to be gloomy to have gloomy people in it." Kita plays it straight: a slime ruining everything is gonzo world, but Kita's reaction is that she feels gross and her clothes are ruined.
- **Combat:** expected, "fairly regular," not the only thing.
- **Scale:** small person somewhere large — "though it doesn't have to be that large to make Kita feel small."
- **Don't stay on Absalom the whole game.** Leaving is part of the point.
- **The emotional contract:** NPCs are **not therapists.** The world isn't hostile to her condition, but it doesn't coddle her either. Her fears should be built on real, smaller problems that she inflates — not come from nowhere because everyone is endlessly understanding. She runs into real difficulty because of who she is. Missed checks are real misses.

**Where it's pointed:** Kita starts alone, drone-first, broke, avoiding everything. A crew comes looking for the builder of Miku; a devil-worshipping cell inside the fintech firm that owns both her contract marketplace *and* her building is building a machine to harvest tenants' souls through lease defaults — and she is one of the tenants it will catch. Pulling that thread is hacking/infiltration work against literal evil, gets her onto a ship, and leads off-station (the Tally's ledgers are mirrored off Absalom). Her arc is the player's to find; the world's job is to keep putting real, modest, inflatable problems in front of her and a non-verbal path next to the verbal one.
